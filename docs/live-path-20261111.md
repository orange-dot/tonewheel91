# Live path: priority, memory, CPU placement, frequency (2026-11-11)

Operator question: "sad me zanima live put i kako bolje da iskoristimo
procesor. mozda visi prioritet, mozda turbo?" This note records the host as
found, the four changes made without root (steps 1–4), the measurements,
and the options left that need root or a larger change (steps 5–7).

## The host as found

- i7-4600U: 2 cores, 4 threads (CPUs 0–1 and 2–3 share a core), 0.8–3.3 GHz.
  `intel_cpufreq` (passive) with `schedutil`. Turbo is on.
- `sched_util_clamp_min_rt_default = 1024`: the kernel already runs
  real-time tasks at full frequency, turbo included.
- tuned with power-profiles (tuned-ppd). The profile is `balanced`;
  `performance` maps to `throughput-performance` (governor `performance`,
  `min_perf_pct` 100, boost on, no C-state limit).
- `ulimit -r 0` and `memlock` 8 MB. `SCHED_FIFO` is refused, and RealtimeKit
  grants priority 20 with `RTTimeUSecMax` 200 ms.
- `intel_idle` C-states down to C10, whose exit latency is 2.6 ms, against a
  2.67 ms period.
- `default` is PipeWire (quantum 1024, minimum 32); the soak ran on the
  internal card through it. The AG06 was not connected.

## What changed (no root, each switchable)

Patchbench reads `PB_LIVE_LOCK`, `PB_LIVE_PIN` and `PB_LIVE_HOLD`; each is on
by default and `0` turns it off.

1. **Measurement in the engine.** The audio thread records a histogram of
   DSP time and of the interval from one period's start to the next (10 us
   bins), the maximum of each, and CPU migrations (`pb_timing`).
   `build/pb_live_soak` (`make check-patchbench-soak`) holds N voices
   silently and reports percentiles, xruns, migrations and the audio CPU's
   frequency sampled every 10 ms.
2. **Memory lock.** The engine, its pools (a pool built for a swap too) and
   the PCM buffers are mlocked, and 256 KB of the audio thread's stack is
   touched and locked before its loop: 1.04 MB measured, against an 8 MB
   limit.
3. **CPU placement.** The audio thread is pinned to the first logical CPU of
   the last physical core. While audio runs, the UI and the probe threads are
   kept on the other core, and are released when it stops
   (`pb_cpu_layout_read`).
4. **Profile hold.** While audio runs, Patchbench holds `performance`
   through `HoldProfile` on the system bus and releases it when audio stops
   or the process exits. It calls RealtimeKit directly as well and reports
   the priority granted.

The bus calls use libsystemd's sd-bus loaded with `dlopen`
(`workbench/pb/pb_host.c`), so the build gains no dependency, and a host
without it just reports the feature as unavailable. The status bar says
what was granted, for example "real time via RealtimeKit 20, cpu 2 alone,
1.0 MB locked, performance profile held".

## A finding first: an overloaded real-time thread was killed

The first matrix (`build/live-soak-20261111-01/`, a browser and a screen
recorder running) lost every 32-voice run to **SIGKILL**. RealtimeKit allows
a real-time thread 200 ms of CPU without blocking (`RLIMIT_RTTIME`). With
DSP above the period, the ALSA write never blocks, and the kernel kills the
process. The Patchbench would have vanished the same way with 32 heavy
voices. Three fixes, all in `workbench/pb/pb_engine.c` and `pb_rt.c`:

- **Soft limit below the hard one.** The soft `RLIMIT_RTTIME` is now
  150 ms, so SIGXCPU arrives first. The handler drops the audio thread to
  `SCHED_OTHER | SCHED_RESET_ON_FORK`: RealtimeKit sets the reset-on-fork
  flag, an unprivileged thread may not clear it, and without it the call
  fails with EPERM, which was why the first version of the handler still
  ended in SIGKILL. The meters and the status bar report the demotion.
- **Overload guard.** If DSP exceeds the period 4 times in a row, or the
  write does not block 40 times in a row (about 107 ms, under the soft
  limit), the engine stops every voice and tail with a pool reset. A musical
  panic would not do: Granica's releases kept the load up and the guard
  fired 45 times in 15 s. Patch, controls and configuration stay. The status
  bar says so and suggests a lower voice limit.
- With both in place, 36 Granica voices (well past what one core carries)
  give one overload stop, 0 xruns and no demotion in 15 s.

## Measurements

Run `build/live-soak-20261111-02/` (`run.sh`; one `.txt` and one histogram
`.csv` per case). The host was quiet (the operator closed everything else).
`default` through PipeWire, 128-frame periods × 3, RealtimeKit priority 20
in every case, Granica held at gain 0, 60 s per case (22 490 periods). "off"
means RealtimeKit only; "on" means lock, pin and hold. "busy" adds three
threads rendering private 8-voice pools, as the probe does after an edit.
DSP times are in ms; the period is 2.667.

| 8 | – | on | 2993 | **0.55** | 0.77 | 0.88 | 1.46 | 2.89 | 0 | 0, 0 |

| 20 | – | off | 2309 | 1.90 | 2.07 | 2.22 | 2.65 | 2.85 | 12 | 0, 0 |

What the numbers say:

- **The typical period gets 22–35 % faster with tuning on.** That is the
  frequency: without the hold, schedutil leaves the core near 2.1–2.3 GHz
  between short real-time bursts; with it, the core sits at 3.0 GHz.
- **The tail does not move.** p99 and the maximum are the same with
  tuning on or off: the slowest periods already run at full frequency.
- **Busy threads cost frequency, not placement.** With the other core
  loaded, the dual-core turbo limit takes the audio core from 3.0 to
  2.6–2.8 GHz whatever the placement. That is the turbo trade-off on this
  15 W part. Pinning removes migrations (0 in every "on" case) and the
  shared caches; it cannot give back the power budget.
- **Wake-ups are punctual.** The worst interval is 2.8–2.9 ms against a
  2.67 ms period in every case but one (5.3 ms once in 60 s, with busy
  threads), and the 3-period buffer absorbed it. The C10 exit latency is not
  showing as a problem here.
- **Capacity of one core:** about 20 Granica voices keep the worst period
  just under 2.67 ms. 24 voices pass p99.9 at 3.8 ms, and 28 or more set off
  the overload guard. Lighter patches carry more (Tepih is about 20 %
  lighter in `bench_ma3_perf`).

## AG03: through PipeWire and direct

The operator plugged in the AG06/AG03 (card 3, USB). PipeWire made it the
default sink; when idle, WirePlumber suspends the node and closes the
device, so `hw:CARD=AG06AG03` opens directly. The run is
`build/live-soak-ag03-20261111/`, with tuning on, Granica and 60 s per case.

| Path | Period | Voices | DSP p50 | p99 | p99.9 | max | worst wake | xruns |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `default` (PipeWire) | 128 | 20 | 1.50 | 2.12 | 2.63 | 3.57 | 3.61 | 0 |
| `hw:CARD=AG06AG03` | 128 | 20 | 1.48 | 2.09 | **2.28** | **3.08** | **3.11** | 0 |
| `default` (PipeWire) | 64 | 8 | 0.28 | 0.33 | 0.41 | 0.46 | 1.51 | 0 |
| `hw:CARD=AG06AG03` | 64 | 8 | 0.28 | 0.31 | 0.41 | 0.78 | 1.48 | 0 |

- At 128 frames the direct path trims the tail (p99.9 2.28 against 2.63 ms,
  worst 3.08 against 3.57 ms): the PipeWire thread no longer shares the
  work. Typical periods are the same.
- At 64 frames (1.33 ms, a 4 ms buffer) 8 voices run cleanly both ways for
  45 000 periods. The direct path also saves PipeWire's quantum and its own
  device buffer in the end-to-end latency.
- The first two attempts at 64 frames ran at 128: a build error in the soak
  tool (a duplicate `period`) was hidden by a filtered make log, so the old
  binary ran. Those files are kept as `*-INVALID-period128*.txt`; the table
  shows only the reruns.

## What next

Steps 1–4 stay on by default: typical periods are faster and there are no
migrations. The tail does not improve; its maximum moves by single periods
either way (8 voices: 1.18 ms off and 1.46 ms on). The data does not make a case for steps
5 and 6 on this host (wake-ups are punctual, and no preemption shows), so
the AG03 runs above (direct and through PipeWire) do not change that.
For live playing, the direct `hw:CARD=AG06AG03` path is the better one when
nothing else needs the card (`WB_AUDIO_DEVICE=hw:CARD=AG06AG03`); a
Patchbench switch for it is a small follow-up. More voices need step 7, with the caveat above: both cores
busy run at a lower turbo, so two cores give less than twice.

## Step 7: voices on two cores

Operator: "ajde tačka 7, glasovi na dva jezgra".

**Core (stays freestanding, no threads).** `ma_voice_pool_render_parallel`
works in runs of up to `MA_POOL_RUN` = 256 frames:

1. **Banks.** Each voice bank of 8 lanes computes the whole run
   (`run_banks`). The sleep test of the per-frame mix (group released, amp
   envelope idle) is applied lane by lane at the same frame, with the
   scatter at that frame.
2. **Mix.** Per frame, in slot order: transition, pan, sum, the sleep
   bookkeeping, groups, then the output front, the rack, master, safety and
   effects.

Phase 1 goes to a host executor (`ma_pool_executor`: run every part once,
any thread, any order, return when all are done). Banks are dealt to the
least-loaded part. `ma_voice_pool_render` is the same call without an
executor. This is the same bits as the old per-frame interleave: voices
share no state, a sleep depends only on the voice and on a group phase that
changes at events, and a group is reset only when none of its slots is
awake. The run state lives in the pool (bank-major, no shared cache lines)
and adds 66 KB; the pool is still below 1 MiB.

**Patchbench.** One worker thread, pinned to the first CPU of the other
core (the UI and the probe move to the remaining CPU), real time through
RealtimeKit, and woken by a futex for each run of 32 frames or more. The
audio thread computes part 0, then spins briefly and sleeps on the futex
for part 1. `PB_LIVE_WORKER=0` turns it off. `PB_LIVE_WORKER_LEAD_US` (default
0) wakes the worker that long before the next period and spins; it was
measured and did not help (below).

**A start race, found and fixed.** The worker first read the run counter
after its own setup, which includes the RealtimeKit call over the bus and
takes a few milliseconds. A run published in that time was taken as already
seen, so the audio thread waited forever and the soak hung. The worker now
starts from generation 0, which `worker_start` sets before the thread
exists, and the audio thread starts only once the worker reports ready.

**Tests.** `test/ma_pool_parallel.c` (`make check-ma-pool-parallel`, in
`make test`) runs 16 cases: Granica and Tepih; limits 8, 20, 32×4 unison
and 64×2; blocks of 128 and 37 frames; with and without a rack; notes
coming and going so that voices fall asleep inside runs. Each case is
rendered five ways: serial, reversed part order with 2 and with 4 parts,
and real threads with 2 and with 4 parts. All give the serial bits. Every
existing pool pin and twin is unchanged.

**Offline split** (an ideal spinning worker, quiet host, three repeats):
16 voices 1.74–1.77× and 32 voices 1.67–1.75× faster. The mix phase costs
0.03–0.05 ms per 128 frames, and the two parts finish within 0.05 ms of
each other. What is missing to 2× is the dual-core turbo limit.

**Live** (`build/live-soak-2core-20261111/`): AG03 direct (`hw`),
128-frame periods × 3, Granica held, tuning on, 60 s per case (22 500
periods). "busy" adds probe-like threads on the remaining CPUs.

- **Capacity: about 20 Granica voices on one core, about 40 on two.** On
  one core, 32 voices set off the overload guard with or without busy
  threads; on two, 32 and 40 voices run for a minute with no stop and no
  xrun.
- 20 voices gain 1.46×, not nearer 2×: three banks (8 + 8 + 4) split 12/8.
  32 voices (four full banks) split evenly.
- With busy threads on the other CPUs, two cores keep 32 voices steadier
  than the quiet case (max 1.82 against 3.01 ms). The busy load keeps the
  worker's core awake and its frequency up; the quiet case has one 3.01 ms
  period in 60 s, absorbed by the buffer.
- The worker ran in every period: 22 500 runs in each two-core case.
- The predictive wake (`PB_LIVE_WORKER_LEAD_US=400`) was tried at 32 voices
  for 10 s each: p50 1.04 against 1.09 ms, but p99.9 2.32 against 1.82 ms.
  It does not help, so it is off by default.

## Options that need root or more work

5. **C-states.** The deepest states take up to 2.6 ms to leave. Two ways,
   both root:
   - map power-profiles' `performance` to tuned's `latency-performance`
     (`/etc/tuned/ppd.conf`: `performance=latency-performance`). The hold
     then also sets `force_latency`, limiting C-states to C1, and nothing in
     Patchbench changes.
   - a udev rule giving the user `/dev/cpu_dma_latency`, held at, say,
     100 us while audio runs.
6. **`SCHED_FIFO` and memlock:** the `pipewire` group (rtprio 70, memlock
   4 GB) or an `audio` limits file.
7. **Voices on both cores.** Done: see "Step 7" above.
