# MA3-PERF stage attribution of the vector engine, 2026-11-06

**Question.** In the [vector render path](ma3-vector-lanes-20261106.md), how
do the cycles of an 8-slot block divide between stages?

**Answer.** The scalar per-voice front end dominates: 35–47% of a block with
BCS off and 58–64% with BCS on. The vector oscillator block is next, at
17–33%. Everything else is single digits: the filter/ladder and 7-tap FIR,
the decimators, the output and effects, and the SoA lane fill. The
gather/scatter, control/pan, back-end and slot-loop overhead is about 1%
each. BCS alone accounts for roughly 1.3 M of a BCS-on block's ticks.

## Method

`driver/probe_ma3_stages.c` (target `$(BUILD)/probe_ma3_stages`, not part of
`test`) includes `src/ma_voice.c` and `src/ma_pool.c` white-box. It re-runs the
vector path (`ma_voice_pool_render` → `pool_tick` → `ma_lanes_tick` →
`ml_kernel`) as a literal copy, with an `lfence; rdtsc; lfence` read between
stages. The one reordering is that the SoA lane fill runs after all front
ends, since filling only copies values. A twin pool renders through the real
API alongside it. The probe requires every frame and the final slot state to
match bit-for-bit, and they matched in all ten cases in both runs.

The workload is the bench's: 8 held slots, wet effects, 128-frame blocks, 256
warm-up blocks, then 1,024 measured blocks per case. A timer read pair costs at
least 27 ticks, and there are 12 + 4 per bank per sample. The instrumented
render takes about 4–6% longer than the untimed twin.

**Host conditions.** Chrome was running during both runs, including on the
measured core's SMT sibling (`top-after.txt`), so the absolute ticks are
inflated. Between the two runs the per-stage shares agree to within 1–3
percentage points, so the attribution below holds but the absolute numbers do
not. Run: `build/ma3-stages-20261106-01/` (`run1.csv`, `run2.csv`).

## Reading

- **The front end is now the bottleneck.** That is everything a voice does
  per sample before the lanes: 31 control smoothers, character, glide, LFO,
  BCS, two envelopes, the Chebyshev prewarp, noise, the source frame, and two
  mozaik and two raster steps. Raster with BCS off has the largest BCS-off
  front end (44–47%), which points at the raster steps. Every part of the
  front end is per-voice and independent across voices, so it is the next
  thing to carry in lanes.
- **BCS is the single largest front-end item.** Front-end ticks rise by about
  1.2–1.4 M per block when BCS is on, against a total of 2.6–3.4 M.
- **The vector kernel is not the problem.** The oscillator block,
  decimators and filter together take 34–51% with BCS off and 26–29% with BCS
  on.
- **The lane fill (2.5–4.7%) is pure packing overhead.** It disappears if the
  front end writes its outputs straight into the lane inputs.
- **The shared work is small.** Output and effects take 3–6%, and the pool
  bookkeeping (gather/scatter, control/pan, slot loop, back end) about 3–4%
  together.
