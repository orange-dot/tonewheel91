# MA3-PERF — 8-slot baseline with hardware counters, 2026-11-06

This is the first baseline at the interim 8-slot target, using the OPT3 voice
source and the counter harness. The steady-state cost is stable to about 0.5%.
The tails are not, and nothing in the workload explains them. At the observed
turbo clock, the median cost of every patch/BCS case is within the 80% target
(Granica with BCS on sits on the boundary). CPU p99 exceeds that target in nine
of ten cases and the deadline itself in four. The result covers no
optimization and makes no live-readiness claim.

Run: `build/ma3-perf-20261106-01/`. Measured 18:30:25–18:34:47 CET on CPU 2.

## Recipe

- Ten cases: Tepih, Lead, Raster, Prizma and Granica, each with BCS off and on.
  All use limit 8, scenario 3 (all eight slots held), wet effects, 48 kHz and
  128 frames. The deadline is 2,666.7 µs; the plan's proposed CPU p99 ceiling
  is 2,133.3 µs.
- One process per case, with counters required. Each process ran two
  in-process passes of 256 warmup and 2,048 measured blocks. There were three
  epochs, with case order forward, reversed, then forward. That gives 12,288
  measured blocks per case and 122,880 in total, plus empty-bracket runs at
  start and end.
- `run-baseline.sh`, `summarize.py` and `tail-analysis.py` in the run
  directory reproduce the run, the table and the tail analysis.

## Reading

1. **The stable quantity is the median cycle count.** Per-case medians move by
   at most 0.55% between epochs. IPC is 1.68–1.94. The implied clock at the
   median is 3.26–3.29 GHz, which is turbo. CPU-time p99, by contrast, moves by
   up to 1,276 µs between epochs of the same case.
2. **The tail does not come from the workload.** The computation is
   deterministic, yet per-block cycle counts barely correlate between passes
   (r = 0.005–0.18). Above-p99 blocks fall on the same block index in only
   0–6% of comparisons. Medians of the first and last tenth of each pass agree.
   The 30–50% excess at p99 and max in user-space cycles therefore points to
   interference: shared-core and cache contention, interrupts and other
   processes. That makes it as much a MA3-LIVE scheduling problem (isolated
   core, idle SMT sibling, priority) as a DSP one (`tail-analysis.txt`).
3. **The budget depends on the clock, and the clock was not locked.** At the
   observed ~3.28 GHz, the deadline is about 8.75 M cycles and the 80% target
   about 7.0 M. All medians (4.39–7.00 M) fit, with Granica BCS on at the
   boundary. At the 2.1 GHz base clock the deadline would be about 5.6 M
   cycles, and six case medians would exceed it: every BCS-on case except
   Raster, plus Prizma and Granica with BCS off. Turbo is not guaranteed
   under sustained or thermal load.
4. **Rough size of the remaining DSP work.** Bringing the worst median to 80% of
   the deadline at base clock needs about 1.56x (7.00 → 4.48 M cycles).
   Absorbing the observed worst p99 at turbo needs about 1.47x
   (10.30 → 7.0 M). These estimates are derived from this one run, not targets
   the plan has accepted.
